# Menu art, readability and desktop presentation research

Reviewed 13 November 2026 for the Wispbound menu and desktop-viewport pass. Sources below are primary platform guidance, the original collaborative game-accessibility guidelines, and a free lesson presented by Apple's design team. No paid-course enrollment or billing pages were used.

The guidance informs this pass; it does not certify the game against every accessibility criterion. The proposed visual treatment, 900 × 405 logical desktop stage and 150% desktop starting zoom are project decisions, not values prescribed by these sources or a claim about Pixel 10 hardware resolution.

## Primary sources and their implications

| Source | Relevant guidance | Application to this game |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [Microsoft XAG 101: Text display](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/xbox/accessibility/xbox-accessibility-guidelines/101) | Readability depends on the visible glyph's body height, face, spacing and contrast. Its size measurements use rendered ascender-to-descender height, not a CSS declaration. It recommends player text scaling while preserving meaning and functionality. | Audit the actual bitmap letters and digits. Use a consistent title / action / explanation / caption hierarchy, keep essential effects out of tiny captions, and retain distinct numeric glyphs. Whole-stage desktop enlargement complements a readable base design; it does not prove text-scaling accessibility. |
| [Microsoft XAG 102: Contrast](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/xbox/accessibility/xbox-accessibility-guidelines/102) | Important standard-size text and informative visual elements should reach 4.5:1 contrast. Inactive text should reach 3:1. Against artwork, test the lowest-contrast part behind the element. Color should have another signifier. | Keep richly illustrated scenery away from quiet text grounds. Preserve readable disabled labels. Reinforce active, selected, owned, locked and unavailable states with outlines, checkmarks, locks or wording as well as color. Do not lower an entire disabled button's opacity until its label disappears. |
| [Microsoft XAG 112: UI navigation](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/xbox/accessibility/xbox-accessibility-guidelines/112) | Menus need consistent structure, interaction methods, logical reading and navigation order, and predictable focus movement. Repeated controls should appear in consistent places. | Keep Back/Menu, the main next action and secondary alternatives in stable locations. Give Continue/New Game a clear order. Closing an inspection should return the player to its originating choice. A rest choice, artifact decision and confirmation should share one interaction language. |
| [Microsoft XAG 117: Visual distractions and motion settings](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/xbox/accessibility/xbox-accessibility-guidelines/117) | Automatic motion, blinking and moving backgrounds can obstruct reading even when the text itself is stationary. Motion should be pausable or removable; opaque text backgrounds can help. | Prefer authored, mostly static pixel scenery behind menus. Use brief action-driven feedback for focus, Equip or Melt; avoid continuously moving labels and competing pulses. Respect reduced motion and keep flight paused while a decision is being read. |
| [Game Accessibility Guidelines: Simple clear text formatting](https://gameaccessibilityguidelines.com/use-simple-clear-text-formatting/) | Distinct letter shapes and unfussy backgrounds improve short text. Longer passages benefit from mixed case, left alignment, clear spacing and bounded line lengths. | Keep pixel identity in headings and symbols, but judge body glyphs by legibility. Explanations use short left-aligned sentences. Artifact descriptions retain a visible trigger, benefit and important limit instead of scattered centered fragments. |
| [Game Accessibility Guidelines: No essential information by fixed color alone](https://gameaccessibilityguidelines.com/ensure-no-essential-information-is-conveyed-by-a-fixed-colour-alone/) | Color works best as reinforcement for a recognizable shape, pattern, symbol or label. Red/green alone is unreliable for many players. | A selected companion gets a check and border, a locked companion a lock, and an owned skill an ownership mark. Equip/Melt retain words and different artwork. Rarity color remains supported by the rarity label and artifact silhouette. |
| [Apple Design: Design great interfaces for handheld games](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/meet-with-apple/243/) | This free design-team lesson covers flexible layouts, legibility, screen shapes and touch input. The transcript warns that simply scaling a layout across different aspect ratios can make controls misaligned, hard to reach, or too small/large. | Use a stable landscape phone stage for desktop play, while retaining responsive layouts and safe areas on real phones. Keep the two flight actions anchored near the right thumb. Fit the desktop stage into available space without cropping its menus or controls. |
| [Valve: Steam hardware compatibility, Deck display requirements](https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/steamhardware/compat#SteamDeckSpecificRequirements) | Valve judges handheld text by reading distance and actual on-screen character height. For Deck at 1280 × 800 it defines a 9-pixel absolute minimum and recommends 12 pixels, with configurable text size and contrast. These are Deck-specific review values. | Use real rendered-size inspection rather than assuming a large desktop screenshot is readable on a handheld. Treat the game's phone-stage benchmark separately from physical display size. Offer desktop zoom, and inspect the smallest relevant phone layout independently. Do not transplant Deck pixel thresholds into CSS as a universal rule. |

The [Game Accessibility Guidelines contrast guidance](https://gameaccessibilityguidelines.com/provide-high-contrast-between-text-ui-and-background/) independently supports placing text over calm, high-contrast grounds and using outlines where art remains behind it. This is especially relevant to outdoor phone use.

## Concrete design decisions for this pass

1. **Build one visual world around a clear main action.** Use an original moonlit woodland/ruin composition, the wisp and a coherent worn-frame language. Put the strongest light and largest action on Continue or New Game. Companion progression sits in a supporting band. Art supplies atmosphere; it should not compete with the words that explain a choice.

2. **Make information hierarchy match the decision.** An artifact's identity, trigger, effect and limit stay grouped. Its Equip/Melt actions remain adjacent. Rest shows the benefit before optional rules. Important explanations receive a readable body style; decorative uppercase labels stay short. Familiar symbols replace redundant labels only when their meaning is already taught.

3. **Give each state more than one cue.** Available choices are bright and framed; selected/owned choices have a persistent mark; locks are explicit; disabled labels remain legible. Use the same rules across companions, skills, rewards and settings. A new decorative surface should not look clickable merely because it has a bright border.

4. **Make the normal URL comfortable on desktop.** Present the game in a centered 900 × 405 logical landscape stage on desktop, initially requesting 150% presentation scale. Clamp the rendered stage to the available browser area, with a visible Fit/zoom control and a retained preference. This is a game-controlled presentation setting, not browser zoom. A narrow sidebar gets a fitted landscape view; a real phone retains native responsive controls and safe-area handling.

5. **Spend motion on useful feedback.** Keep body text and decision grounds still. Let an equipped artifact travel into its slot, show a short selection response, and let the world art breathe through restrained lighting. Do not animate every menu surface continuously. Preserve the user's pause/countdown/resume flow.

## Verification targets

- Check visible glyphs, including small numbers and 3/5/6/8 distinctions; CSS `font-size` alone is insufficient evidence.
- Check important normal text at 4.5:1 and inactive labels at 3:1 against the actual ground, including art's brightest patch.
- Review phone landscape at 900 × 405, 740 × 333 and 720 × 280. Check notches/safe areas on device; a desktop frame cannot certify them.
- Review the normal root URL on desktop at 1920 × 1080, 1440 × 900, 1280 × 720 and a narrow sidebar. Confirm centering, Fit behavior, saved zoom and no clipped action buttons.
- Check focus order and return behavior, active/owned/locked states without relying on hue, and reduced-motion presentation.
- Compare menu imagery and text together; a contrast swatch or empty-screen layout alone cannot reveal overlap and visual competition.

## Free further learning

Apple's [handheld-interface lesson and transcript](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/meet-with-apple/243/) is the most directly relevant practical session for this project. The Xbox [guideline index](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/xbox/accessibility/guidelines) and the Game Accessibility Guidelines [full list](https://gameaccessibilityguidelines.com/full-list/) provide a continuing review checklist.
