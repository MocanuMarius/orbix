## Owned Deck

Every real earned or forged card enters the owned Deck automatically, with its individual identity, rank and remaining uses. The three-to-five ordered active slots select which owned cards grant abilities. Equipping into an occupied slot stows its previous card; Unequip also returns a card to reserve. Neither action pays a Melt return or refills fuel or charges. Duplicate cards remain separate instances. Existing mounted hands retain their order and uses on upgrade; an unresolved legacy earned card moves into reserve once.

New card journeys equip the chosen Common card first and a guaranteed Lift Guide second, leaving one slot free. The two advertised choices exclude Lift Guide, and its included thumbnail is visible before choosing. Existing journeys keep their cards; loading or melting a starter never creates another. An unchosen legacy starter can still commit its previous choice, including an old Lift Guide offer, without duplicating the Guide.

The Deck shows the active slots and browsable owned cards. Select mode can mark multiple reserve or mounted cards. Its combined Melt preview identifies the exact cards, active abilities lost and total Stored Sparks; cancel is reversible, and confirmation removes the complete validated selection once. Improvements retain their normal prices and never refill uses. Deck is fully editable throughout the active run, including a safely paused flight. The visible flight Deck control and pause menu lead to the same editor. Closing uses the existing pause and tap-to-resume flow. The card receipt offers Continue and Open Deck rather than forcing immediate Equip or Melt. Forge is available inside Deck and from a camp shortcut: Rare costs 30 banked Stored Sparks, Legendary costs 60 with a Rare equipped, and no Forge uses a camp action. Each confirmation buys its exact seeded offer once, then leaves Forge open with the next distinct offer and updated wallet. Melt several cards, return to Forge and continue while affordable. Unbanked flight earnings remain unavailable until clear.

The catalog contains **71 cards: 33 Common, 24 Rare and 14 Legendary**. The [stacking matrix and ordered row rules](skill-cards.md#copy-repeat-and-lifetime) make numeric duplicates and copy compatibility explicit. Matching events resolve from left to right; multipliers use the bonus already earned in that pass, and Refrain shares one replay from slot 1. The retained flight receipt shows both passes without creating another pickup or wave. Named family evolutions preserve real instances and remaining uses.

Field Impulse originals and adjacent compatible Echo/Chameleon sources extend one swipe to **0.8 seconds × effective source count × ring strength**, with the normal force in every direction and one ring spend. Two sources last exactly twice as long; nine supported sources reach 7.2 seconds at a full ring. Packing, Flash recovery and Grow capacity also add compatible numeric sources; Guide, Rhythm, Cache permissions and Second Ember's extra-action limit stay single. Rare Edge Reach enables proximity waves and adds 50% of normal reach per original or Chameleon source. Its help compares the same actual flight scene before and after the card.

Tap a gallery card to read its full face: the name sits on the card above its artwork, with concise rules in the card's lower band. Remaining uses and an explicit improvement label replace the ambiguous rank fraction. Rhythm adds a small first-note/second-note score and a guided third-note window during flight. Misses and completed trios clear the score; its timing follows the shared world clock and slows with the flight. The [Rhythm preview](https://mocanumarius.github.io/orbix/rhythm-preview.html) runs the production evaluator and score.

The main game opens Cards by default with New Game, Continue, Tutorial and Settings. Settings holds audio, teaching, the glossary and relic almanac; Advanced contains the separate-save legacy skill-book override, test tools and Refresh build. The home screen no longer requires a mode choice. Existing Cards and legacy save keys remain separate.

`card-workbench.html` opens a meaningful in-memory sample Deck with active and reserve cards. Real Equip, Unequip, reorder, Improve, reward and Melt actions operate on the sample. Reload resets it; neither journey save is read or written. `cards.html` remains a compatible Cards launcher. Both launchers support installed-worker upgrades, explicit desktop/native display choices and prepared offline play. The two-ink Collector remains the default; the gallery's living tow preview remains available.

The Collector’s eligible field pickups carry a mint green eyelet under the shell. It stays attached to the Spark as Collector catches it; only the loaded tow grows a short filament rooted at that Spark. The gallery includes a native production-renderer comparison of marked Spark/Flash and locked Cache/Dark pickups, plus a playable actual tow clip.

The sixteen card scenes use one 1252 × 1252 WebP atlas (685,406 bytes), generated with the built-in image tool and optimized into sixteen equal 313-pixel cells. The exact prompt and generation provenance are in [the asset prompt record](../dist/assets/prompts.txt); [the complete atlas](../dist/assets/cards/skill-card-scenes.webp) shows the ordered artwork.

## Shipped behavior

- `cards.html` is a fresh installed-phone entry. One tap finishes the release download and opens the normal New Game menu with `?cards=1`. Phone routing stays native; desktop routing retains the landscape host. Explicit `collectorArt`, `display` and `zoom` choices survive the launcher. After the complete download, the launcher and pilot work offline; an unprepared offline visit asks for a connection.
- The pilot has a separate save and starter seed. It starts with three ordered slots and one of two Common gifts. Each first real clear awards one card; Equip/replacement/Melt and checkpoint improvements preview the actual effects, charges and Stored Sparks transaction. The final guardian's card becomes a victory trophy. Standard play retains the skill book.
- Every pilot map offers two optional reachable Rest forks. Recover, Add slot and Forge share the normal one-action allowance. Mounted Growth Seed unlocks Grow; Second Ember spends its charge only on a successful second distinct eligible action. Forging previews the exact seeded card and costs both Stored Sparks and a Rest action. Add slot and Forge grant no healing or refill.
- Lift Guide is an optional Common starter and a temporary loan during card-mode First Light's Lift lesson. Rare Rhythm consumes each successful even trio of manual Lifts for one Luminance wave. Three Resonant Harvest rarities reward natural uncollected Spark crossings without collecting them or paying from generated objects. Echo and Chameleon use the real immediate-right source, including its shared finite charges. Field Impulse remains uncopyable.
- Common Tight Packing joins the 16-card pool as a fixed-rank mounted passive: “Pack 25% more Stored Sparks. Every 4 you pack earns 1 extra.” Actual overflow/exit packing earns the bonus, with integer quarter carry across successful clears and saves. Failed/Dream attempts cannot farm it; flat rewards, Melts, gifts, pact payouts and other unrelated grants retain their normal value. Chameleon can borrow the passive; Echo has no action to repeat. Card reward and final victory show the matching flight's named bonus receipt, with no stale/Forge receipt.
- Collector delivery/discard handling cues appear in both modes. The separate art gallery has matched full-app films, native crops and front/behind/fetch stills. Its two- and three-ink candidates are opt-in for that visit; the current four-tone Collector remains the default. Gallery play links pass through the update launcher.
- The Spirit Fire mineral rim's grain, hot patches and specks stay attached to world pixels. A narrower inward band makes it quieter. Rendering spends 4.5 reach units per unit of rock instead of 3, making penetration one third shallower while preserving the open-air law and dynamic physical shadows.
